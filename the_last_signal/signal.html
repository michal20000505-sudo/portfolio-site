          01 — The Source

          A supergiant of roughly eighteen solar masses, observed through 4,812 years of intervening dust. With extinction removed, its convection cells resolve to 41 km. The core failed; the photosphere had nine minutes left and did not know it. The transmission left the system 71 hours before the light did.

          We received its final message 4,812 years after its death.

          02 — The Transmission

          Eleven minutes and forty-two seconds of structured carrier across four usable bands: 1420.1 (carrier), 1420.4 (primary), 1420.9 (harmonic), 1421.6 (artefact). Recovered content includes incomplete phrases — …still receiving?, we cou—, the water is, …if anything is left… — numerical sequences, unresolved glyph groups, and timestamps that precede any epoch we hold.

          03 — Fragments

          Fragment 07 — Atmospheric recording. Four minutes of an atmosphere moving. Wind, most probably. Twice, something crosses the microphone and the level drops.

          Fragment 12 — Unknown orbital structure. Periodic dimming that no natural body accounts for. Thin, wide, and held in an orbit too circular to be debris.

          Fragment 19 — Repeating biological pattern. A lattice that repeats at prime intervals and then, without warning, stops repeating.

          Fragment 24 — Final translated sentence. The last complete grammatical unit in the transmission. It arrives after eleven minutes of silence and is not repeated.

          Fragment 31 — Unverified visual reconstruction. Nineteen percent of this image is recovered. The rest is the archive imagining what it wants to see.

          04 — Decoding

          Corrected for frequency (1420.4 MHz), carrier phase (137°) and receiver alignment, the final sentence resolves as:

          “We looked for you until the light ran out.”

          Nothing follows it. The carrier runs for another nine seconds and stops.

          05 — Afterlight

          The source is gone. The message remains.

          A reply transmitted from this receiver would reach the coordinates in approximately 9,624 years. There would be nothing there to receive it.

          Sound

          The optional audio layer is synthesised in the browser and carries no speech. It consists of a low sustained bed around 38 Hz, filtered noise standing in for receiver hiss, an amplitude-modulated carrier tone during Chapter 02, and occasional struck metallic resonances that decay over several seconds. Chapter 04 is deliberately near-silent. No information is conveyed by sound alone.
