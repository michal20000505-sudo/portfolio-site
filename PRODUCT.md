# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Polish small-to-medium businesses needing web design, branding, or print work. Business owners and decision-makers who want a site or visual identity and are evaluating whether Michał is someone they can trust and work with. They browse on a range of devices, often without design literacy — they're judging feeling and credibility, not technique. The site must convert them from curious to convinced.

## Product Purpose

Michał Jarosiński's creative portfolio — spanning web design, branding, graphic design, and game development. Active since 2014. The site exists to generate direct client inquiries from Polish SMBs. Success is a visitor browsing work, feeling like Michał is a capable and approachable person to hire, and reaching out.

## Brand Personality

Creative, playful, human. Warm energy, personality-forward. The person behind the work should be visible — not hidden behind a polished agency facade. The work is technically ambitious (glitch effects, rubber-band text, particle systems), but the overall impression should feel like a human made it with care, not a studio machine produced it.

Note: the current CMYK-neon aesthetic reads as electric and technical. Future design decisions should pull toward warmth and approachability without abandoning the technical confidence that signals craft.

## Anti-references

- Generic Webflow templates: scroll-animation-heavy, whitespace-maximal, serif-headline aesthetics seen on thousands of freelancer sites. No anonymous minimalism.
- Corporate portfolio: conservative, formal, case-study-heavy, no personality. Michał is not a faceless agency.

## Design Principles

1. The person is the brand — Michał's personality should be tangible throughout, not confined to an "About" section.
2. Approachable confidence — the work should impress without intimidating; a Polish SMB owner should feel like they can actually hire this person.
3. Earned spectacle — interactive effects must feel purposeful and crafted, not decorative performance for its own sake.
4. Show, don't explain — copy stays tight; the portfolio images carry the argument.
5. Cohesive voice across disciplines — web, print, branding, and game work should feel unified by personality, not fragmented by category.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal WCAG compliance target. Basic usability: keyboard navigable, screen-readable structure, sufficient contrast for most users. Reduced-motion handling should be maintained (current site partially implements this on mobile).
